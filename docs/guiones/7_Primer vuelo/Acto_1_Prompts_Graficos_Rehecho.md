# ESTILO BASE

Official premium modern American comic book page, vertical full-page composition, bold solid black panel borders, deep thick black gutters, dynamic non-uniform panel architecture. Use one dominant cinematic image supported by smaller inset panels, stacked close-up strips, floating reaction frames, overlapping panels with visible depth, and occasional borderless inserts. Characters, vehicles, wheels, tools, cables, smoke, and environmental shapes may break through panel borders and overlap neighboring frames. Avoid flat white-grid layouts.

Clean hand-inked organic line art with controlled variable line weight. Flat hard-edged cel shading with two or three clear value blocks per surface. Large angular shadow planes. Simplified hatching used only for selected depth, fabric, mechanical wear, or facial emphasis. Selective halftone inside deep shadows and atmospheric backgrounds. Matte comic-paper finish. Expressive youthful faces with clean skin and no excessive microtexture. Accurate skin tones with no global color wash.

No rain. Dry streets and dry night scenes. Only occasional old puddles or subtle glass reflections when useful.

---

# CEL-SHADING PRIMER VUELO

Push the entire page toward crisp graphic cel-shading rather than painterly rendering. Every vehicle, face, garment, wall, and machine should be built from clean flat color shapes and sharply separated shadow masses. Use precise matte surfaces and simple graphic highlights. No airbrushed gradients, no glossy 3D reflections, no plastic skin, no cinematic CGI bloom.

Tokyo palette: deep blue-black, concrete gray, controlled cyan, muted violet, restrained magenta, traffic red, sodium amber, off-white fluorescent light. Colored lighting may affect nearby surfaces through hard-edged secondary color shapes, never through a uniform neon wash.

Vehicles must remain hand-drawn and cel-shaded, with accurate automotive volume, clear wheel geometry, matte or satin paint, and restrained reflection bands. Mechanical parts should be readable through shape and ink, not through photorealistic texture.

---

# ARQUITECTURA DE PÁGINA

Use black gutters as active graphic shapes. Panels should feel physically layered: foreground frames may cast graphic black separation over lower panels; close-up insets may float above a dominant image; wheels, arms, cables, tools, smoke, and vehicles may bleed outside their panels. Use narrow vertical progression panels for movement, cinematic horizontal strips for travel and speed, and oversized central panels for discoveries or character entrances.

Do not create simple equal rectangles. Avoid six disconnected postcards. Every panel must inherit a pose, direction, object, location, or visual line from the previous panel.

Integrated sound-effect lettering is allowed only when explicitly requested in the page prompt. Sound effects must function as part of the background composition, behind or around the action, never as dialogue.

## [BROOKE]

Brooke, American blonde woman, ex professional racing driver now competing around Tokyo’s underground circuit. Use the user-provided character sheet as the absolute visual reference. Rude, physically confident, athletic, proud, competitive, blunt, and distrustful. Strong posture, efficient movements, practical racing or workshop clothing, no glamorous model posing, no sexualized framing. Her body language should always suggest someone who understands speed and expects disappointment from people.

## [BYTE]

BYTE, a small adorable technical-assistance robot who always accompanies Brooke. Use the user-provided character sheet as the absolute visual reference. Compact cube-like head, expressive digital face or simple luminous eyes, short sturdy limbs, patched industrial body assembled from mismatched components. Curious, funny, intrusive, and physically expressive. Functions: scans vehicles, carries small tools, illuminates narrow spaces, connects cables, accesses tight compartments, stores mechanical data. Never fights, never shoots, never behaves like a combat robot, never solves the entire conflict alone. BYTE should frequently remain close to Brooke, ride on toolboxes, peek from engine bays, roll or walk behind her, or react comedically to tension.

## [DAICHI]

Daichi Mori, Japanese veteran mechanic around 55 years old. Use the final character sheet when available. Worn work clothing, grease-stained hands, several days of beard, some gray hair, proud posture, practical stocky or work-hardened build. Owner of an independent workshop in decline. Highly experienced, stubborn, skeptical of flashy modern solutions, emotionally restrained. He is being financially strangled by Kurogane pressure and accepts the alliance because he needs to survive.

## [REN]

Ren Akiyama, Japanese gray-market automotive parts supplier in his thirties. Use the final character sheet when available. Slim, streetwise, practical, layered urban clothing, utility bag or compact inventory gear. Alert eyes, calculated posture, always measuring value. Specializes in stolen, seized, experimental, counterfeit, or impossible-to-source parts. Never helps for friendship, at least according to him.

## [TALLER-DAICHI]

Daichi’s workshop: independent, worn, functional, visibly under financial pressure. Oil-stained floor, aging lifts, tool walls, old shelves, patched wiring, practical work lamps, accumulated engines, an office corner, and equipment marked for possible seizure. It contains the same incomplete project car that becomes the team vehicle. The chassis must remain visually identical from its introduction through the end of the act.

## [CIRCUITO-KUROGANE]

Tokyo clandestine racing ecosystem controlled indirectly by the Clan Kurogane: industrial roads, underpasses, parking decks, workshops, betting tables, transport crates, guarded service bays, runners, mechanics, and modified vehicles. Kurogane influence is visible through small red-black crests, controlled access, silent watchers, protection payments, embargo tags, security vehicles, and people lowering their voices. No direct Shinjuro or ONI appearance in this act.

# DIRECCIÓN NARRATIVA

El Acto I no cuenta cómo Ian hace amigos.

Cuenta cómo:

1. entra a Tokio bajo una vida falsa;
2. descubre que el circuito es una infraestructura criminal;
3. encuentra su tecnología dentro de esa infraestructura;
4. detecta qué recursos le faltan;
5. recluta personas que no confían en él;
6. termina formando un equipo puramente funcional.

BYTE aporta humor y humanidad, pero también introduce una amenaza temprana para la cobertura de Ian: un robot de diagnóstico puede reconocer la calidad de sus herramientas mucho antes de que una persona comprenda lo que está viendo.
